Christian Cairo
(AD 970-1200)


The first church was built in Fustat in the 670s, by the native Christian inhabitants of Egypt after the Arab conquest. Fustat was established just to the northeast of the Byzantine fortress of Babylon, and it was in the older walls that the Christian (and indeed the Jewish) population remained throughout Cairo's medieval history.

Fustat had a bishop, as did Babylon; the head of the Egyptian church was the patriarch of Alexandria (one of the five most important bishops in the entire Christian world), but in ___, the patriarchate was moved to Cairo. The Coptic Christians of Egypt had mostly followed the monophysite doctrine (that Christ has one nature) since the fifth century, but another Christian group, the Melkites (who believed that Christ has two natures), followed the patriarchate of Constantinople. By the time of the Fatimid conquest, there were over 20 churches.

The Christian community was diverse, and include both rich merchants and officials, and poor laborers from the countryside.
