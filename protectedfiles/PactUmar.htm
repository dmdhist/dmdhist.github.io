The Pact of Umar

 

After the rapid expansion of the Muslim-ruled territory in the 7th century, Muslim leaders were required to work out a way of dealing with non-Muslims, who for centuries remained in the majority in many areas.  The solution was to develop the notion of the "dhimma", or "protected person".  The dhimmi were required to pay an extra tax, but usually they were unmolested. 

 

The Pact of Umar is supposed to have been the peace accord offered by the Caliph Umar II (682-720) to the Christians of Syria, a "pact" which influenced the pattern of later interaction.  Its authenticity has been doubted by scholars, who think that it was written by later lawyers and attributed to an earlier era, but certainly many of the provisions in it were put in place under the early Muslim rulers.  In fact, some parts of it are very similar to provisions in Roman law for the treatment of non-Christians!

 

 

 

In the name of God, the Merciful and Compassionate. This is a letter to the servant of God Umar [ibn al-Khattab], Commander of the Faithful, from the Christians of such-and-such a city. 

 

When you came against us, we asked you for safe-conduct (aman) for ourselves, our descendants, our property, and the people of our community, and we undertook the following obligations toward you:

 

We shall not build, in our cities or in their neighborhood, new monasteries, churches, convents, or monks' cells, nor shall we repair, by day or by night, such of them as fall in ruins or are situated in the quarters of the Muslims.

 

We shall keep our gates wide open for passersby and travelers. We shall give board and lodging to all Muslims who pass our way for three days.

 

We shall not give shelter in our churches or in our dwellings to any spy, nor hide him from the Muslims.

 

We shall not teach the Qur'an to our children.

 

We shall not manifest our religion publicly nor convert anyone to it. We shall not prevent any of our kin from entering Islam if they wish it.

 

We shall show respect toward the Muslims, and we shall rise from our seats when they wish to sit.

 

We shall not seek to resemble the Muslims by imitating any of their garments, the cap, the turban, footwear, or the parting of the hair.  We shall not speak as they do, nor shall we adopt their surnames.

 

We shall not mount on saddles, nor shall we gird swords nor bear any kind of arms nor carry them on our persons.

 

We shall not engrave Arabic inscriptions on our rings.

 

We shall not sell fermented drinks.

 

We shall shave the fronts of our heads.

 

We shall keep to our own style of dress wherever we may be, and we shall bind the girdle round our waists

 

We shall not display our crosses or our books in the roads or markets of the Muslims. We shall use only wooden clappers [i.e. not bells] in our churches very softly.  We shall not recite our services in a loud voice when a Muslim is present.  We shall not carry Palm branches [on Palm Sunday] or our icons in procession in the streets. We shall not raise our voices when following our dead, nor carry candles on any of the roads of the Muslims or in their markets. We shall not bury our dead near the Muslims.

 

We shall not take slaves who have been owned by Muslims.

 

We shall not build houses overtopping the houses of the Muslims.

 

(When I brought the letter to Umar, may God be pleased with him, he added, "We shall not strike a Muslim.")

 

We accept these conditions for ourselves and for the people of our community, and in return we receive safe-conduct.

 

If we in any way violate these undertakings for which we ourselves stand surety, we forfeit our covenant [dhimma], and we become liable to the penalties for contumacy and sedition.

 

Umar ibn al-Khittab replied:  Sign what they ask, but add two clauses and impose them in addition to those which they have undertaken. They are: "They shall not buy anyone made prisoner by the Muslims," and "Whoever strikes a Muslim with deliberate intent shall forfeit the protection of this pact."
