Orthodox Cathedral and Baptistry

According to legend, Ravenna's first bishop was Apollinaris, a disciple of St. Peter, who came to Ravenna around 70 AD.  Archaeological evidence shows that there were Christians around Ravenna only from the end of the second century, around 170 AD.  The first definite mention of a bishop of Ravenna comes from the year 343.

According to tradition, the cathedral was built around 400, thus right at the time that the city became the capital of the western Roman empire.  The cathedral existed in its original form until the sixteenth century, when it was destroyed and replaced by a new structure.

The baptistry, a building used specifically for baptism (remember that in this period, adults rather than infants were more likely to be baptised), was built a few decades later, in the 430's.  It was octagonal in ground plan, a traditional form for baptistries.  The baptistry survives with its original decoration intact.
