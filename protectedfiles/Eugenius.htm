Pope Eugenius III, April 11, 1147, Divini dispensatione II

 

This is a papal bull, or a public communication issued by a pope.  A slightly earlier bull (Divini dispensatione I) declared the Second Crusade to Jerusalem; this one announces a similar undertaking in the Baltic.

 

 

 

Among the other princes and potentates, certain kings who rule over other nations, having taken up the sign of the life-giving cross, prepare to go to the aid of the eastern church, and with the aid of God dispose themselves to fight powerfully against the enemies of the cross of Christ, who, committing many sins, have cruelly killed in those parts our brothers in Edessa and in many other places. 

 

The king of the Spanish is mightily armed against the Saracens of those parts, over whom now through the grace of God he has often triumphed. 

 

And finally there are those among you who desire to become participants in holy labor and reward, and who prepare to go against the Slavs and other pagans living in the North, and to subjugate them, with the aid of God, to the Christian religion.  

 

We, attentive to their devotion, by the authority of almighty God and blessed Peter prince of the apostles conceded by God to us, grant that remission of sins which our predecessor of blessed memory Pope Urban instituted for those going to Jerusalem to all those who did not take up the cross of Jerusalem, but decided to go against the Slavs, and to remain in consideration of devotion in that expedition, as has been stated. 

 

We prohibit by this authority under penalty of excommunication, that no one might accept any money or other ransom from those pagans, whom he might subjugate to the Christian faith, so that he might permit them to remain in their perfidy.

 

 

 

**************************

(translated D. M Deliyannis)
