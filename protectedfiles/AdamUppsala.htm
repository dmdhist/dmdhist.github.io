The Heathen Temple at Uppsala

Adam of Bremen

 

Adam of Bremen wrote a history of the archbishops of Bremen, in Germany (Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum) between 1072 and 1076, using both oral and written sources., This selection, from book 4, sections 26-27, refer to events apparently taking place in Adam's own day, in Sweden, whose church was subject to the archbishop of Bremen.

 

 

 

Now let us say a few words about the superstition of the Swedes.  That people has a most noble temple at the place called Uppsala (Ubsola), located not far from the city of Sigtuna (Sictona).  In this temple, which is completely covered with gold, the people worship the statues of three god:  the most powerful of them, Thor, has his throne in the middle of the space, and on either side are placed Wotan and Frey (Fricco).  Their significance is as follows:  Thor rules the air, and governs thunder and lightning, wind and rain, fair weather and crops.  Next Woden, which means "furor", wages war, and distributes strength to men against their enemies.  The third is Frey, who bestows peace and pleasure to mortals.  His statue is made with an immense phallus.  Wodan is depicted armed, like our Mars; Thor looks like Jupiter with his scepter.

 

The people also worship men made gods, whom they endow with immortality because of their great deeds, as it says in the Vita of St. Ansgar, they did in the case of King Eric.

 

For all their gods they have appointed priests to offer sacrifices for the people.  If plague and famine threaten, they sacrifice to Thor; if war, to Woden; if weddings are celebrated, to Frey. 

 

Every nine years they celebrate in Uppsala a festival common to all the provinces of Sweden.  No-one is exempt from this festival.  The kings and the people, all and each send their gifts to Uppsala, and what is more cruel than any punishment, those who now have taken on Christianity redeem themselves through these ceremonies. 

 

The sacrifice is thus.  Of every living thing that is male, nine heads are offered, whose blood is said to placate the gods.  The bodies are hung in a grove which is next to the temple.  For this grove is so sacred to the pagans that its individual trees are believed to be divine because of the death and putrefaction of the victoms.  For there dogs and horses are hung up with men; a 72-year-old Christian told me he had seen their bodies hung up together.  About the many and wicked songs, which they use in their offering ritual, I think it better to be silent.
