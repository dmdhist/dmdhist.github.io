Selections from "The so-called 'Canons of Edgar'"


(taken from Councils and Synods with other documents relating to the English Church, vol. I, A.D. 871-1204, part 1, A.D. 871-1066, ed. D. Whitelock, M. Brett and C. N. L. Brooke, Oxford, 1981)

 

According to Bede, the English converted to Christianity between 597 and 660, and Christianity flourished.  However, with the Viking invasions, many churches and monasteries, especially in northern England, were destroyed.  Recovery was slow, but in the later 10th century there was a major push by church leaders to restore ecclesiastical discipline, and many documents survive that testify to the ways that the church was trying to do this.

 

One of the leaders of the reform movement was Wulfstan, who in his career served as Bishop of London (996-1002), Bishop of Worcester (1002-1016), and Archbishop of York (1002-1023).  He wrote sermons, and also was an advisor to the kings of England, both the Anglo-Saxon king Aethelred II (978-1016) and the Danish king Cnut, who ruled England from 1016-35.  Wulfstan drafted law-codes for these rulers, possibly including the one from which selections are given below.

 

This text is known as the "so-called 'Canons of Edgar'" because one manuscript attributes them to King Edgar of England (959-75), but it is known that they were written by Wulfstan between 1005 and 1008. 

 

I have given you only the first 26 chapters (and note that some of the numbers are missing); the remainder are concerned with the responsibilities of priests.  I have included some of the Old English in which these laws were originally written, but I have to say that it was a bit tedious to type, so I didn't include them all!

 

 

1.  It is right that priests zealously serve and minister to God and intercede for all Christian people; and that they all always be loyal and obedient to their master, and all be unanimous for the common benefit; and that each be a support and help to others in religious and secular concerns; and that they also be loyal and faithful to their secular lords according to God's law.

Riht is þæt preostas beon geornlice Gode þeowiende 7 ðeniende 7 for eall Cristen folc ðingiende; 7 þæt hig ealle beon a heora ealdra holde 7 gehyrsume, and ealle anræde to gemænre þearfe; 7 þæt ælc sy oðrum on fultume 7 on helpe ge for Gode ge for worulde; 7 þæt heo beon heora woruldhlafordum eac holde 7 getrywe æfter Godes rihte.

 

2.  And it is right that each honor the other; and the juniors are readily to obey their elders, and the elders readily to love and teach their juniors.

7 riht is þæt ælc wurðie oðerne; 7 hyran þa gingran georne heora yldran, 7 lufian 7 læran þa yldran georne heora gingran.

 

5.  And it is right that priests announce in a synod if anything is harming them, and if any man has seriously ill-used them.  They are then all to take it on as if it were done to them all, and to give their help that it may be amended as the bishop directs.

7 riht is þæt preostas on sinoðe gecyðan gif heom hwæt derige, 7 gif heom ænig man healice misboden hæbbe.  Fon hi þonne ealle on swylce hit hym eallum gedon beo, 7 gefylstan to þæt man hit gebete swa se bisceop getæce.

 

6.  And it is right that every priest announce in a synod if he knows in his parish any man disobedient to God, or evilly fallen into deadly sins, whom he cannot turn to repentance or dare not because of worldly power.

 

 

7.  And it is right that no dispute which is between priests be referred to the arbitration of laymen, but their own fellows are to arbitrate and make peace, or they are to refer it to the bishop if one needs must.

 

 

8.  And it is right that no priest of his own freewill desert the church to which he was consecrated, but he is to keep it as his legal wife.

 

Note:  by this time the church was insisting on having a celibate priesthood, so the church was considered the priest's "wife".

9.  And it is right that no priest take away from another any of the things which belong to him; neither in his church, nor in his parish, nor in his guild, nor in any of the things which belong to him.

 

 

10.  And it is right that no priest receive the scholar of another without the permission of him whom he previously followed.

 

 

11.  And it is right that every priest in addition to learning learn a handicraft.

7 riht is þæt preosta gehwylc toeacan lare leornige handcræft georne.

 

12.  And it is right that no learned priest scorn the half-learned, but improve him, if he know better.

7 riht is þæt ænig gelæred preost ne scende þone samlæredan, ac gebete hine gif he bet cunne.

 

13.  And it is right that no highborn priest despise the more lowly born; for if one considers it rightly, then all men are of like birth.

7 riht is þæt ænig forðboren preost ne forseo þone læsborenan; forðam gif hit man ariht asmeaþ, þonne syn ealle men anra gebyrda.

 

14.  And it is right that every priest provide for himself rightly, and not be a trader or covetous merchant.

7 riht is þæt preosta gehwylc tylige him rihtlice, 7 ne beo ænig mangere mid unrihte ne gytsiende massere.

 

15.  And it is right that every priest grant baptism and confession as soon as it is asked for, and announce everywhere in his parish that each child is to be baptized within seven days, and that no-one is to remain too long unconfirmed.

7 riht is þæt preosta gehwylc fulluhtes 7 scriftes tyðige sona swa man gyrne, 7 æghwær on his scriftscyre beode þæt ælc cild sy gefullod binnon vii nihtum 7 þæt ænig man to lange unbiscopad ne wyrðe.

 

16.  And it is right that every priest zealously teach the Christian faith and entirely extinguish every heathen practice; and forbid worship of wells, and necromancy, and auguries and incantations, and worship of trees and worship of stones, and that devil's craft which is performed when children are drawn through the earth, and the nonsense which is performed on New Year's Day in various kinds of sorcery, and in heathen sanctuaries and elder trees, and in many various delusions in which men carry on much that they should not.

7 riht is þæt preosta gehwylc Cristendom geornlice lære 7 ælcne hæþendom mid ealle adwæsce; 7 forbeode wyllweorðunga, 7 licwigelunga, 7 hwata, 7 galdra, 7 treowwurðunga 7 stanwurðunga, 7 ðone deofles cræft þe man dryhð þær man þa cild þurh þa eorðan tihð, 7 ða gemearr þe man drihð on geares niht on mislicum wigelungum 7 on friðsplottum 7 on ellenum, 7 on manegum mislicum gedwimerum þe men on dreogað fela þæs þe hi ne sceoldan.

 

17.  And it is right that every Christian man zealously accustom his children to Christianity, and teach them the "our father" and creed.

 

 

18.  And it is right that one abstain on feast days from heathen songs and games of the devil.

 

 

19.  And it is right that one abstain from Sunday marketing.

 

 

20.  And it is right that one abstain from senseless garments and shameful training of the hair.

 

 

22.  And it is right that every man learn so that he knows "our father" and creed, according as he wishes to be buried in a consecrated grave or to be entitled to the sacrament; for he who will not learn it is not truly Christian, nor ought he by rights to stand sponsor to another man at baptism or at confirmation, until he learn it.

 

 

23.  And it is right that there be no strife between men on feast-days and appointed fast-days.

 

 

24.  And it is right that one abstain from oaths and ordeals on feast-days and appointed fast-days.

 

 

25.  And it is right that every man abstain from his wife at feast-seasons and appointed fast-seasons.

 

 

26.  And it is right that priests maintain the churches with all reverence for divine ministry and for pure service, and for no other thing; nor are they to allow any foolishness inside or in the neighborhood:  neither idle speech, nor idle deed, nor foolish behavior, nor ever anything idle; and that no dog nor horse come within the churchyard, still less a pig, as far as one can prevent it.
