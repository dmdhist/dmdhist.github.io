Alwalton Manor, 1279

The Hundred Rolls are the records of a government survey carried out under King Edward I in the 1270s to determine the ownership and value of land for taxation and judicial purposes, just as the Domesday Book did in the 1080s.  A Hundred is a geographic and administrative division used in England from the Anglo-Saxon period throughout the Middle Ages; the survey was organized according to these divisions. 

For each manor, the Hundred Rolls record the names of the lord of the manor, the names of the manor or township, the feudal tenures and obligations, the economic resources (arable land, enclosed pastures, etc.), number of tenants, amount of land held by tenants, names of each tenant, and tenants’ obligations.

This is an account of the manor of Alwalton, Huntingdonshire from the Hundred Rolls of 1279.

 

The abbot of Peterborough holds the manor of Alwalton and vill from the lord king directly; which manor and vill with its appurtenances the lord Edward [the Confessor], formerly king of England gave to the said abbot and monastery of that place in free, pure, and perpetual alms.

And the court of the said manor with its garden contains one half an acre.  And to the whole of the said vill Alwalton belong 5 hides and a half and I virgate of land and a half, of which each hide contains 5 virgates of land and each virgate contains 25 acres. Of these hides the said abbot has in demesne I hide and a half of land and half a virgate, which contain as above. Likewise he has there 8 acres of meadow. Also he has there separable pasture which contains I acre. Likewise he has there three water mills.  Likewise he has there a common fish pond with a fish-weir on the bank of the Nene, which begins at Wildlake and extends to the mill of Newton and contains in length 2 leagues.  Likewise he has there a ferry with a boat.

Free tenants.
Thomas le Boteler holds a messuage with a courtyard which contains I rood and 3 acres of land, by charter, paying thence yearly to the said abbot 14 shillings.

Likewise the rector [priest] of the church of Alwalton holds I virgate of land with its appurtenances, with which the said church was anciently endowed. Likewise the said rector has a holding the tenant of which holds I rood of ground by paying to the said rector yearly 12 pence.
 
And the abbot of Peterborough is patron of the church.

Villeins.
Hugh Miller holds 1 virgate of land in villenage by paying thence to the said abbot 3 shillings I penny.  Likewise the same Hugh works through the whole year except I week at Christmas, I week at Easter, and I at Whitsuntide, that is in each week 3 days, each with I man, and in autumn each day with 2 men, performing the said works at the will of the said abbot as in plowing and other work. Likewise he gives I bushel of seed wheat and 18 sheaves of oats for fodder for animals. Likewise he gives 3 hens and I cock yearly and 5 eggs at Easter. Likewise he does carrying to Peterborough and to Jakele and nowhere else, at the will of the said abbot. Likewise if he sells a brood mare in his courtyard for 10 shillings or more, he shall give to the said abbot 4 pence, and if for less he shall give nothing to the aforesaid. He gives also merchet and heriot, and is tallaged at the feast of St. Michael, at the will of the said abbot.

There are also there 17 other villeins, viz. John of Ganesoupe, Robert son of Walter, Ralph son of the reeve, Emma ate Pertre, William son of Reginald, Thomas son of Gunnilda, Eda widow of Ralph, Ralph Reeve, William Reeve, William son of William Reeve, Thomas Flegg, Henry Abbott, William Hereward, Serle son of William Reeve, Walter Palmer, William Abbot, Henry Serle; each of whom holds I virgate of land in villenage, paying and doing in all things, each for himself, to the said abbot yearly just as the said Hugh Miller.

There are also 5 other villeins, viz. Simon Mariot, Robert of Hastone, Thomas Smith, John Mustard, and William Carter, each of whom holds half a virgate of land by paying and doing in all things half of the whole service which Hugh Miller pays and does.

Cottars.
Henry, son of the miller, holds a cottage with a croft which contains 1 rood, paying thence yearly to the said abbot 2 shillings. Likewise he works for 3 days in carrying hay and in other works at the will of the said abbot, each day with I man and in autumn I day in cutting grain with I man.

Likewise Ralph Miller holds a cottage with a croft which contains a rood, paying to the said abbot 2 shillings; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise William Arnold holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 2 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Hugh Day holds a cottage with a croft which contains I rood, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Sara, widow of Matthew Miller, holds a cottage and a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the said abbot 4 pence; and she works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Sara, widow of William Miller, holds a cottage and a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 4 pence; and she works just as the said Henry.

Likewise William Kendale holds a cottage and a croft which contains I rood, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Agnes the widow holds a cottage with a croft which contains I rood, paying thence yearly 12 pence; and she works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Geoffrey Note holds a cottage and croft which contains half a rood; paying yearly 8 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Beatrice the widow holds a cottage and croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and she works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Henry of Aylingtone holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, and I acre of land, paying to the abbot 2 shillings 8 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Benedict Atelane holds a cottage with a croft which contains a half rood, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Geoffrey Baker holds a toft and croft, and it contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 4 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Robert Prenk holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the said abbot 4 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Roger Doket holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 4 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Geoffrey Drake holds a cottage with a croft and which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 4 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Sara the widow holds a cottage with a croft which contains a rood, and a half acre of land, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and she works just as the said Henry.

Likewise William Drake holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 6 pence; and he works just as the said Henry.

There are there also 6 other cottars, viz. William Drakejr., Amycia the widow, Alice the widow, Robert son of Eda, Willam Pepper, William Coleman, each of whom holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying and doing in all things, each for himself, just as the said William Drake.

Likewise William Russel holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying to the abbot 8 pence; and he works in all things just as the said Henry Miller.

There are moreover there 5 other cottars, viz. Walter Pestel, Ralph Shepherd, Henry Abbot, Matilda Tut, Jordan Mustard, each of whom holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, paying thence and doing in all things to the said abbot just as the said William Russel.

Likewise Beatrice of Hampton holds a cottage and croft which contains 1 rood, paying to the abbot 12 pence; and she works in all things just as the said Henry.

Likewise Hugh Miller holds 3 acres of land paying to the abbot 42 pence.

Likewise Thomas, son of Richard, holds a cottage with a croft which contains half a rood, and 3 acres of land, paying to the abbot 4 shillings, and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise Ralph Reeve holds a cottage with a croft which contains I rood, and I acre of land, paying to the abbot 2 shillings; and he works just as the said Henry.

Likewise each of the said cottages, except the widows, gives yearly after Christmas a penny which is called head-penny.

 

From J.H. Robinson, trans, Translations and Reprints from the Original Sources of European History, published for the Dept. of History of the University of Pennsylvania (Philadelphia, University of Pennsylvania Press, 1897), vol III: 5, 4-8.
 

 

Glossary*

acre (acra, ager):  A value of land assessment used often for pasture, meadow and woodland. Measurement of an acre as a multiple of hides varies from region to region; by custom, the amount of land that one plow team could plow in a day

cottar (cotarius): poorest class of unfree peasant, usually holding little or no land

croft:  enclosed garden plot adjacent to a village house

demesne:  land farmed by peasants, whose produce was given directly to the lord of the manor.

heriot:  payment made on the death of a tenant, usually the "best beast".

hide (hida): measurement of land for tax assessment; approximately 100-120 acres, depending on local variations in the acre. 

hundred (hundredum): an Anglo-Saxon administrative unit of territory, subdivision of a shire.  Originally it referred to an amount of land that supported a hundred men-at-arms. 

manor (manerium, mansio): Equivalent to a single holding, with its own court and probably its own hall.

merchet (merchetum):  payment made to the lord to obtain permission for a daughter to marry

messuage:  a plot of land with a dwelling-house

pence (denarius):  silver pennies, the smallest unit of coin

reeve (praepositus, praefectus): a manor overseer/supervisor, one of the villagers, appointed by the lord or elected by the peasants.

rood (roda):  a quarter acre.

shilling (solidus): coin worth twelve pennies.

tallage:  an annual tax levied at will by a lord on peasants

toft:  a house-site with a small yard

vill:  an administrative subdivision of a Hundred

villein (villanus): wealthiest member of the unfree peasant class; tenant who occupied lands on condition of performing services for the lord of the manor.

virgate (virgata, virga):  a quarter of a hide.
