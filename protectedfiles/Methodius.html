St. Clement of Ohrid, Life of St. Methodios (trans. P. Stephenson)

 

Methodius and his brother Cyril were born in Thessalonike, Greece, in the 810s-820s, to a noble family.  The city had many inhabitants who spoke Slavic, as well as Greek speakers, and it seems that the brothers knew both languages.  They were well-educated at the university in Constantinople, and lived in the city as monk-scholars; by 860 were well-enough known that they were chosen to go on a mission to the Khazars.  In 862, Prince Rastislav of Moravia asked the emperor and patriarch to send Christian missionaries to convert his subjects, and Cyril and Methodius were chosen.  They decided to translate the Bible and the liturgy into Slavic (now known as Old Church Slavonic), so that people could understand it, and they created an alphabet, now called Glagolitic, to write the language.

 

As missionaries they were successful, but other missionaries from Germany disapproved of them, and especially of their Slavic-language Church, so in 867 Pope Nicholas I invited them to Rome, where he officially approved the Slavic liturgy.  Cyril died at Rome, but Methodius was given the title Archbishop of Sirmium, and went back in 869.  He quarrelled with the German Archbishop of Salzburg, who tried to remove him, but he kept the support of the popes, and worked to establish the Slavonic liturgy, until his death in 885.

 

This biography was written by Methodius' friend and follower St. Clement of Ohrid (Bulgaria).

5.  And so it came to pass that the Slavic princes Rastislav and Svetopolk of Moravia sent envoys to Emperor Michael, and they said to him "We are here by the grace of God.  Many teachers of Christianity have come to us from Italy, from Greece and from German lands, and they all teach differently.  But we Slavs are simple people and do not have among us a man to instruct us in the ways of truth and to explain to us the meaning of the scriptures.  Therefore, good master, send us such a man, capable of guiding us in the ways of every truth."

 

Then the emperor said to Cyril the Philosopher, "Do you hear these words, philosopher?   None but you can go and do this work.  Therefore take many gifts, and go there, and take your brother the Abbot Methodios. Because you come from Thessalonike, like everyone from there you speak a pure Slavic tongue."

           

And they dare not refuse God and the emperor, according to the words of the apostle Peter who said "Hear God, honor the emperor" [I Pet., 2: 17].   Having heard the high imperial order, they devoted themselves to prayer, along with those who were of the same spirit as they.  Then God gave the philosopher in revelation the Slavic script, and he, after arranging the alphabet, composed the gospel sermon and started on his way to Moravia, taking Methodius.  And after three years had passed they returned from Moravia, having taught disciples there.

 

6.  When he learned of these two men, the Apostolic Father, [Pope] Nicholas sent for them, wishing to see them as much as the angel of God.  He consecrated their teachings, placing the Slavic Gospels on the altar of St. Peter the Apostle.  He also consecrated the blessed Methodius to the priesthood.  But there were many other people who reviled the Slavic Scriptures, saying:  "Except for the Jews, Greeks and Latins, it is improper for any people to have their own writing according to the inscription which Pilate wrote on the Lord's cross."  Calling those people Pilatists and trilinguists, the Apostolic Father condemned them....

 

7. [Cyril dies]

 

8.  And Kotsel, sending envoys to the pope, asked him to let Methodius, our blessed teacher, return to him.  The pope replied, "Not only to you, but also to all those Slavic lands do I now send him forth to be the teacher on behalf of God and St. Peter, the first-enthroned and the key holder of the kingdom of heaven." And he sent him forth, writing the following epistle:

 

Hadrian, bishop and servant of the Lord [i.e. the pope], to Rastislav, Svetopolk and Kotsel . . . You requested a teacher not only from this holy see, but also from the faithful emperor Michael.  And he sent to you the blessed philosopher Cyril along with his brother, whereas we failed to do this.  And when they learned your countries belonged to the Apostolic see, they did nothing contrary to canon, but hastened to us, bringing the relics of St. Clement.  Deriving threefold joy therefrom, we considered the matter and decided to send to your lands our blessed son Methodius, an orthodox man accomplished in mind, whom we consecrated with his disciples in order to teach, as you requested and fully explain in your language the Scriptures and holy mass, that is, the liturgy and baptism according to all the rules of the church, just as Cyril the Philosopher had begun through the grace of God. . . .

 

Kotsel received Methodius with great honors and sent him again to the pope, along with twenty reverend men, to ordain him Bishop of Pannonia on the throne of St. Andronicus, apostle, and one of the Seventy; and so it came to pass.

 

9.  And after this, the old enemy, the envier of good and adversary of truth [i.e. the devil] inclined the heart of the Moravian king and all the bishops toward the enemy [the Franks] and against Methodius.  And they said to him, "You are teaching in our lands."  Then he answered, "If I had known they were yours [i.e. Frankish], I would have stayed far away; but they are Saint Peter's.  Verily if you, out of jealously and greed, transgress canon for the old boundaries, and prohibit God's teachings, beware, lest you spill your brains wishing to penetrate an iron mountain with a skull of bones."  They said to him with bitterness, "You shall come to evil."  He answered, "I speak the truth before the emperor and am not ashamed.  But you work your will against me.  For I am no better than those who lost their lives through many torments for speaking the truth."   . . .  However, they banished Methodios to Swabia, and detained him for two and a half years.

 

10.  Word of this reached the Apostolic Father.  Having found out, he sent an interdict against them, that all the king's bishops not celebrate the mass, that is, the liturgy, for as long as they detained Methodius.  Thus, they released him, saying to Kotsel, "If you keep this man with you, you shall not get off lightly with us."

           

. . . And it came to pass then that the Moravians drove out all the German priests who were living among them, sensing that they accepted them not, but forged discord against them.  And they sent to the Apostolic Father, saying, "Since our fathers once received baptism from Saint Peter, give us Methodius as our archbishop and teacher."

           

And the Apostolic Father sent him at once.  And Prince Svetopolk and all the Moravians received him.  They entrusted to him all the churches and clergy in all the cities.  And from that day forth, God’s teachings and the clergy grew greatly and multiplied in all the cities.  And for that reason the Moravians began to grow and multiply, and the pagans to believe in the One God, casting aside their lies.  And Moravia began to expand much more into all lands, and to defeat its enemies successfully, as they themselves are always relating.

 

11. There was also prophetic grace in him, so that many of his prophecies came true. We will recount a couple of them.

           

A pagan prince, mighty and strong, who was based in Visla, mocked Christians and did evil to them. Methodius sent word to him saying: "It would be well for you my son to receive baptism voluntarily in your own land, lest you be baptized by force in another land; for then you will think of my words." And so it came to pass.

 

On another occasion, when Svetopolk was waging war against the pagans and had no success, but was only being delayed, on the approach of Mass, he received these word from Methodius: "If you promise me that on the holy day of St. Peter you will come with your warriors and spend the day with me, I trust that God will forthwith deliver your enemies into your hands." And so it came to pass.

15.  Then, leaving aside all the worldly noise, and casting his anxieties on God, he set to work with two priests from among his disciples, both fast copyists, and quickly translated from the Greek into the Slavic language all the books of the Bible in full, except for Maccabees, in the course of six months, starting in March and ending on 26th October. When finished, he gave due praise and glory to God, who alone give such grace and speedy success . . . .
