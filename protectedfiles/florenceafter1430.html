Florence after 1430



Finally, by 1430's, internal politics began getting out of hand again.  At this time a family rose to prominence who would dominate Florence for the next two centuries. 

The Medici had originally been merchants, especially of textiles.  They bought raw wool, distributed it to Florentine spinners, brought the thread from spinners to weavers, bought the cloth and sold it.  In the 1390's they established a bank in Rome, which grew to be largest in Europe, and the Medici became enormously wealthy.


Medal of Cosimo de Medici the Elder, c. 1465-69, labelled
"decreto publico p(ater) p(atriae)" ("by public decree, father of his country").

Cosimo de Medici was the head of one of the political factions in Florence.  Cosimo used his vast wealth to buy support, subsidized the military, filled offices with his supporters, built churches, provided public entertainments, and sponsored magnificent works of art and architecture.  He, then his son and grandson, controlled Florence from 1434-94.  They didn't do away with republic, just controlled it.  Although the Medici were briefly kicked out in 1494-1512, they returned to power; in 1537 they were declared dukes.  But by then we are out of the Middle Ages altogether.
