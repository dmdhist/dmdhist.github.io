The Salic Law (selections)

It is thought that Germanic society outside the Roman empire, which did not have a tradition of writing, ran according to customs and laws that were learned orally from generation to generation.   When the Germanic tribes entered the former Roman Empire, some Germanic kings had law codes written down, in imitation of Roman written law, from the 7th century on.  The codes are very different in concept from Roman law; the foundation was a system of payments for crimes.  Under this system, when one was convicted of a crime, one had to pay the victim a certain amount of money based on the crime and the status of both the victim and the perpetrator (the payments were called 'composition' for injuries, and 'wergeld' = 'man-value' for homicide).  Supposedly this system was designed to eliminate the concept of blood feuds, which otherwise seem to have been the method by which people avenged themselves of crimes.

The Franks were the people who entered what is now France in the 470's, and set up a kingdom under their leader Clovis around 486.  This law code was supposedly composed under Clovis himself.  A very similar lawcode was written in Anglo-Saxon England about a hundred years later; thus, you can assume that the society described in this lawcode was similar to the one at West Stow.

Note:  a solidus (plural solidi) was a gold coin of the Roman Empire; there were, according to the code, 40 denarii in a solidus.  The value of a solidus is difficulty to determine, but it was roughly equivalent to the cost of 1/2 to 1 year's worth of bread for one person. In other words, it was a lot of money!

In court, proof was determined by oaths of people summoned to support one side or the other in a case (they were not witnesses, but swore to support the oaths of the plaintiff and defendant), or by ordeal, usually of plunging one's hand into boiling water and seeing what happened.  It is not clear how long one had to pay a convicted fine, but if you could not pay, the offender was placed outside the law, and his property and person became liable for the debt (i.e. he could be enslaved).  But, if a man by himself didn't have enough property to pay a composition, his entire family could be asked to help, up to the sixth degree (by the same token, an entire family shared the composition that was paid to one of their members).

Title IX. Concerning Damage done among Crops or in any Enclosure.

1. If any one finds cattle, or a horse, or flocks of any kind in his crops, he shall not at all mutilate them. If he does so and confesses it, he must restore the full value of the animal to its owner and keep for himself the weakened animal that he had struck.  But if he does not confess it and it is proved against him, he shall be liable to pay 600 denarii, that is 15 solidi, in addition to return of the animal, plus a payment for the time its use was lost.

Title X.  Concerning Stolen Slaves or other Chattels

1.  He who steals another man's male or female slave, horse, or mare, and this is proved against him, shall be liable to pay fourteen hundred denarii, that is 35 solidi, in addition to returning the chattel stolen plus a payment for the time its use was lost.

2.  If the male or female slave has carried some property of his or her lord with him or her, the thief shall restore the bondsman and the property and is liable to pay 6000 denarii, that is 15 solidi.

3.  He who steals or kills or sells or set free another man's male slave, and it is proved against him, shall be liable to pay 1400 denarii, that is 35 solidi.

4.  He who steals a female slave shall be liable to pay 1200 denarii, that is 30 solidi.

5.  The freeman who takes another man's slave with him while committing a theft or conducts any business with him shall be liable to pay 600 denarii, that is 15 solidi.

6.  He who loses [i.e. steals and sells] a female slave worth fifteen or twenty-five solidi or a swineherd, vine dresser, metalworker, miller, carpenter, or groom, or any other craftsman worth 25 solidi, and it is proved against him, shall be liable to pay 2880 denarii, that is 72 solidi, in addition to return of the slave.  He who steals or kills or sells an overseer, steward, butler, horsekeeper, groom, a metalworker, goldworker, or carpenter or swineherd of household servant worth 25 solidi shall be liable to pay 1400 denarii, that is 35 solidi, in addition to return of the slave plus a payment for the time his labor was lost.  If it is a female overseer or female household servant worth 25 solidi, the above provisions should be observed.

7.  He who steals a young male or female household slave shall return the 25 solidi value of the slave and in addition shall be liable to pay 1400 denarii, that is 35 solidi, and return of the slave plus a payment for the time his/her labor was lost.  He who steals a slave who keeps young horses shall be liable to pay 1800 denarii, that is 45 solidi.

Title XI.  Concerning Thefts or Housebreaking Committed by Freeman

1. If any freeman steal, outside of the house, something worth 2 denarii, he shall be sentenced to 600 denarii, which make 15 solidi, in addition to return of the object stolen plus a payment for the time its use was lost.

2. But if he steal, outside of the house, something worth 40 denarii, and it be proved on him, he shall be sentenced, besides the amount and the fines for delay, to 1400 denarii, which make 35 solidi.

3. If a freeman break into a house and steal something worth 2 denarii, and it be proved on him, he shall be sentenced to 1200 denarii, that is 30 solidi.

4. But if he shall have stolen something worth more than 5 denarii from a locked enclosure, and it have been proved on him, he shall be sentenced, besides the worth of the object and the fines for delay, to 1400 denarii, which make 35 solidi.

5. But if he have cut or duplicated a key and thus have entered the house and stolen anything from it, he shall be sentenced, besides the worth of the object and the fines for delay, to 1800 denarii, which make 45 solidi.

6. And if he have taken nothing, or have escaped by flight, he shall, for the housebreaking alone, be sentenced to 1200 denarii, which make 30 solidi.

Title XII. Concerning Thefts or Housebreakings on the Part of Slaves.

1. If a slave steal, outside of the house, something worth two denarii, he shall, besides paying the worth of the object and the fines for delay, be stretched out and receive 120 blows or shall pay 120 denarii, that is 3 solidi, to spare his back.

2. But if he steal something worth 40 denarii, he shall either be castrated or pay 240 denarii, that is 6 solidi.  But the lord of the slave who committed the theft shall restore to the plaintiff the worth of the object and the fines for delay.

Title XIII. Concerning the Abduction of Freemen or Free Women

1. If three men carry off a free born girl from her house or workroom, they shall be compelled to pay 1200 denarii, that is 30 solidi.

2. If there are more than three, each one shall pay 200 denarii, that is 5 solidi.

3. Those who shall have been present with arrows shall be sentenced to 120 denarii, that is 3 solidi.

4. But the abductor shall be compelled to pay 2500 denarii, which make 62.5 solidi.

5. But if they have carried off that girl from behind lock and key, or from the spinning room, they shall be sentenced to the above price and penalty.

6. But if the girl who is carried off be under the king's protection, then the fine shall be 2500 denarii, which make 62.5 solidi.

7. But if a servant of the king, or a half-freeman, should carry off a free woman, he shall make composition with his life [i.e. shall be given as a slave to the family of the girl].

8. But if a free woman has followed a slave of her own will, she shall lose her freedom.

9. If any body take another man's female slave and joins her to himself in matrimony, he shall remain in servitude with her.

10.  He who associates another man's half-free woman with himself in marriage shall be liable to pay 1200 denarii, that is 30 solidi.

Title XVI. Concerning Arson.

1.  He who sets fire to another man's house while men are sleeping therein and it is proved against him shall by 62.5 solidi to him whose house it is, and as many men as were in the house should summon him to court concerning the seolandouefa and to each of them he is liable to pay 62.5 solidi.  And if anyone was burned inside, he shall be liable to pay 62.5 solidi plus the composition of 8000 denarii, that is 200 solidi; and to him whose house it is he shall be liable to pay 2500 denarii, that is 62.5 solidi.

2.  He who sets fire to a building made of wattle and daub and it is proved against him shall be liable to pay 2500 denarii, that is 62.5 solidi.

3.  He who sets fire to a barn or granary with grain in it and it is proved against him shall be liable to pay 2500 denarii, that is 62.5 solidi [plus the value of the grain].

4.  He who sets fire to a sty with pigs in it or a stable with cattle or hay in it and it is proved against him shall be liable to pay 2500 denarii, that is 62.5 solidi, in addition to restoration of the value involved plus a payment for the time their use was lost.

5.  If a Roman inflicts one of these things on another Roman and proof is not certain, he may clear himself with twenty oathhelpers, half of whom he has chosen.  If he cannot find the oathhelpers, then he must go to the ordeal of boiling water.  If he is judged guilty, he should pay 1200 denarii, that is 30 solidi.

6.  He who sets fire to another man's fence or hedge and it is proved against him shall be liable to pay 600 denarii, that is 15 solidi [plus the value of the hedge or fence].

7.  He who cuts down another man's fence or hedge shall be liable to pay 600 denarii, that is 15 solidi, in addition to restoration of the value involved plus a payment for the time its use was lost.
