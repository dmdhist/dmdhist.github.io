Meet with Prof. Deliyannis in this class meeting (signup for a time will be posted in Canvas announcements) to discuss your research topic and faculty consultant.

Jan. 27    Using library resources 
In-class discussion about, and work on bibliographies

               Assignment (due Feb. 3):  Upload to Canvas a preliminary bibliography for your research project.  You should certainly have consulted your faculty consultant about this.  The bibliography should be formatted correctly using either MLA or Chicago format.
Your bibliography must contain, at a minimum:
         - one scholarly monograph
         - eight articles published in academic history journals (if in doubt, ask)
         - any other resources that are useful
         - primary sources consisting of a database or a large set of texts or images

Feb. 3    Making your own research work plan and strategy
In class you will produce a research work plan, based on the way that you know you work. It should be fairly detailed: what do you think you will do each week, and why? You will not be held to this.

               Assignment (due Feb. 10):  This assignment should be done on Powerpoint or an equivalent, and uploaded to Canvas.  Make a slide for each of your primary sources, with the following information:
         - what it is
         - how you are accessing it (i.e. book, online, archive, etc.)
         - what language was the original, how you are reading it
         - who wrote/made it, when, where
         - why it was made
         - what its presumed audience was
On a second slide, put a representative sample (a paragraph, a newspaper item, an image) to share with the class

Feb. 10   Primary sources
In-class discussion of primary sources; presentation of your primary source

               Assignment (due Feb. 17):  pick a scholarly article from your bibliography, and upload its PDF to Canvas "Discussions" -> "Scholarly article upload", give the PDF the name "YourLastName".

               Assignment (due Feb. 17):  read the article that you uploaded, and complete the Canvas assignment "Scholarly article analysis", in which you note:
         - the thesis
         - the primary sources from the footnotes
         - whether the author is arguing against historical ideas from other historians
         - whether you think the argument is coherent
You will do the same thing with another student's article, in class.

Feb. 17    Secondary sources
In-class discussion of scholarly articles, based on your assigned readings

               Assignment (due Mar. 3):  make an appointment to meet a second time with your faculty consultant, and submit a report by Mar. 3.

Feb. 24    Meet with consultant, work on gathering and reading your research materials
***No in-class meeting***

               Assignment (due Mar. 3):  Upload to Canvas an annotated bibliography of everything on your list, both primary and secondary sources.  For each item, you write brief notes on what it is about, and how you think it will help you to write your paper.  Naturally, this means that you must have read or at least skimmed the items!

Mar. 3    Historical context
In-class discussion about the historical context of your projects:  what's the scope, what kinds of information do you need to include?  Individual meetings with the remaining time.

               Assignment (due Mar. 10):  prepare an oral book report about one scholarly monograph on your bibliography.  In it you should briefly explain (a) what the subject and thesis of the book are; (b) whether you can tell if the author is arguing against other theories; (c) what primary sources the author uses in the book; and (d) whether you found it informative and convincing.  Your book oral report should take should take 5-8 minutes, thus requires some details (quotes) to supplement these points.  THERE IS NO WRITTEN COMPONENT TO THIS ASSIGNMENT.
