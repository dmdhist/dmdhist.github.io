One Credit Courses - Rationales

a.  Status quo (T100)
Pro:
- T100, led by the DGS with the help of six faculty, focuses on useful supplemental content for majors.
- T100 is enjoyable, easily-earned high grade for majors.
- T100 builds community among majors.
- The one credit for T100 is helpful for some majors in meeting requirements.
Con:
- For some majors, one credit is an unhelpful “orphan”.
- Course is not as rigorous as regular 3-credit offerings.
- Participation in course is extra effort for faculty.

b.  More one-credit courses like T100
Pro:
- Additional one-credit courses such as “Historical Perspectives on Current Events,” “War and History,” historical fiction, historical video games, and collective mini-research projects would provide more supplemental content for majors would have advantages noted above.
- The courses would make it easier for students to take the equivalent of a three-credit course toward degree requirements.
- Like T100, the courses would build community among majors.
- Additional one-credit courses would represent greater commitment to majors.
Con:
- Additional one-credit courses would enlarge less-rigorous portion of departmental offerings.
- The courses would be difficult to staff on a voluntary basis.

c.  One-Credit Term Paper Add-On
Pro:
- Students would develop skills in research and writing by preparing a one-credit term paper for any non-seminar History course.
- Students would work more closely with faculty.
- Often unable to take seminars in their areas of interest, majors could still do research in those areas in lecture courses.
- Majors could use the one-credit add-on to explore paper topics for J400 and J450.
- Like other one-credit courses, the term paper add-on would help students accumulate useful credits toward graduation.
- The term-paper add-on would boost department’s reputation for innovation.
Con:
- Faculty teaching lecture courses would have to be willing to advise students and grade their papers.

d.  One-credit career course
Pro:
- Majors will benefit from a one-credit course that helps them to understand the professional and educational uses of the history degree and to prepare resumes and applications.  This class could draw on alumni, graduate students, and Arts and Sciences Career Services.
- This course will emphasize the practicality of the history major.
- This course will build community among majors.
- This course will represent an enhanced commitment to our majors.
- While there are a number of similar courses in other disciplines, the career course will be an innovation in history and would heighten the department’s profile.
Con:
- As with other one-credit courses, this one presents a challenge with staffing.

e.  Require Three One-Credit Courses
Pro:
- The requirement will give students more variety in their coursework and more flexibility in completing the major.
- If students do the term paper add-on one or more times, they will become more proficient in research and writing.
- Faculty can serve as the instructor for three one-credit courses in lieu of teaching one three-credit course.
Con:
- If students take three one-credit, easy-A courses, the major will be watered down.
- The requirement will reduce enrollments in lecture classes.
- The requirement will exacerbate staffing issues, particularly with the voluntary faculty oversight of one-credit term papers.
- Allowing faculty to count three one-credit courses as the equivalent of a three-credit course will create oversight problems for the chair of the department.
