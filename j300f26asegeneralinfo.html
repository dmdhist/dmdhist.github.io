Description

When the Roman legions pulled out of Britain in the early fifth century, the island entered a "dark age", during which groups of people from the continent entered Britain and became its rulers, and their language and culture became dominant.  Eventually, their territory was called Angle-land, or England.  In this course, we will examine the history and culture of England from 400 to 1066.  We will consider a variety of issues such as the question of the scope of the migration/invasion, the history of Christianity in England, social and political development, contacts with other islands and the continent, the Vikings, the gradual unification of the island, and finally the the Norman conquest of 1066.  We will read extensively in both primary and secondary sources, and we will also examine archaeological material in detail.


WRITING IS THINKING. You may wonder why there is still an Intensive Writing requirement in the age of AI, since computers can do your writing for you?  The university is committed to helping you learn how to do your own thinking, and writing is, by definition, a product of thinking - and it is how you convey your thoughts and ideas to others.


Thus, in this class we will be spending time talking about the way that History is produced and written.  In addition to examining English history, we will be discussing historical methodology and historical writing, both through through discussion of books and articles written by modern historians about the topic, and in your written assignments.  We will pay particular attention to the way that scholars use the available primary sources to construct an argument.


In this class, you will read a lot, write short response papers about readings, produce a 5-7-page paper that is re-written after feedback, do a book report that is both oral and written, and write a longer term paper on a topic that fits the description of the class.


Learning outcomes

A successful student will demonstrate, by the end of the semester:

• knowledge of the history of England from 400-1066.

• the ability to evaluate and compare scholarly arguments.

• the ability to produce a historical argument based on different types of appropriately selected primary and/or secondary sources.

• the ability to present analysis and research in written form.

• the ability to present historical information and make historical arguments orally.

Requirements

This course will be taught as a seminar; that is, classes will be discussion-based, and students should expect to participate actively in debate and discussion.  It is crucial that you do the readings for each class before you come to class, otherwise very little will make sense.


This class will be electronic-device-free.  There is really no reason to have an open laptop in a discussion class - there are no exams, so you don't need to take notes (although you are welcome to do so on paper).  The one reason would be to look at what you wrote in the reading response as we discuss the readings, but if you want to have those in front of you, I recommend that you print them out and bring them to class.


A CASE Intensive Writing class has the following requirements: write at least 5000 words, not counting revisions, response statements, reflection papers, annotated bibliographies, etc. At least one paper must be redrafted in light of the professor's commentary.


All writing assignments should be submitted using 12-point font, double-spaced with 1" margins, no extra line space between paragraphs. Doing this results in about 300 words/page. Thus, 5000 words is about 17 pages of writing. Your words will be counted, to make sure that you have written enough for IW credit. Using extra-wide margins, large font, extra spacing between paragraphs, etc. will not fool anyone.

Attendance and participation: Attendance will be taken each class.  You are allowed two FREE ABSENCES - i.e. they will not count against your attendance grade.  If you are sick or have another kind of emergency absence, please send me an email and your absence will be excused (i.e. not be counted as one of your free absences).  Participation means having done the assigned reading for the day, having formed opinions about it based on the set response question for the day, and taking part in discussion.


Response questions: Starting on Sept. 1, most class days have an assigned reading response question.  There are a total of 16, of which the first four and the last one are required.  You must complete a total of ten of them; thus you may choose to skip six of the non-required ones.  The purpose of these responses is to ensure that you have read the assigned readings and are ready to discuss them.  If you submit a response, you will be expected to participate in the discussion on that day.  You are to answer this question or questions in note-style form (i.e. you do not need complete sentences, but you must answer them completely) in the Assignments section of Canvas.  THEY MUST BE ANSWERED BEFORE THE START OF CLASS ON THE DAY FOR WHICH THEY ARE ASSIGNED.  These will be graded from 0-2 points.  2 points means that it is clear that you read the full reading and thought about it; 0.5 point means that it's not clear that you read it all, or that you gave it much thought.  I suggest that you plan your schedule carefully, including the other paper assignments and book reports, so that your workload is evenly distributed; I also suggest that you do the earlier ones in case you are sick or have some other emergency.  Your grade for this component of the class will be calculated as a percentage out of 20 (2.0 points x 10 responses).


Short paper: Each student will write a 5-7 page paper on a question to be announced in class on the Workday on Sept. 24.  In that class meeting, I will bring photocopies of all the readings up to that point, and you may also bring photocopies or any other printed materials that you like, but you may not bring any electronic devices.  You will identify 10 quotes from whichever sources you want that you will use to answer the assigned question, and you will write these on a piece of paper and email a photo of it to me.  You will then have one week to write a 5-7-page paper that uses all of these quotes, submitted in Canvas on Oct. 1.  You are required to use AT LEAST 10 QUOTES (you can use more if you like) from all the materials assigned for class (referenced, with footnotes), but you are not expected to do any outside reading for this paper.


This paper will be returned to you, with comments and a grade, on Oct. 8.  You are required to modify your paper on the basis of these comments, and submit the revised version and the original version (with the comments) on Oct. 15.  If you have addressed all of the comments appropriately, then the grade that you get for the original version will be an A, and the grade on the revised version will be whatever you have earned.  If you do not address the comments in the revised version, then the original grade will stand and the revised paper will get its own grade.


Book report: Each student will read, summarize, and briefly present to the class one book report on a scholarly book, which will form part of the bibliography for your term paper.  I will assign the dates after the term paper topics are finalized. Your oral book report should take between 3 and 5 minutes (practice! and be concise); you may use only one 3x5 note card.  DO NOT SUMMARIZE THE BOOK'S CONTENTS.  Instead, you should briefly explain (a) what the subject and thesis of the book are; (b) what the chapter organization is; (c) whether you can tell if the author is arguing against other theories; (d) what primary sources the author uses in the book; and (e) whether you found it informative and convincing.  Your written book report should be three pages long, and should discuss the same topics as the oral report.  (just as a note: AI does a terrible job with these book reports, sometimes describing a different book altogether.  DO YOUR OWN WORK!)  Written book reports must be turned in to Canvas on the day that they are presented in class.


Term paper: Your term paper must be 9-12 pages (12-point font, double spaced, 1" margins).    Description of the topics can be found below; you must choose one of these topics.  The week you have been assigned a topic, you will meet with Prof. Deliyannis outside of class.   She will provide you with a bibliography that includes primary and secondary sources; you must read all of these items and use them in your paper.


Annotated bibliography: In class on Oct. 20, we will do a library check to make sure you can obtain all the items on your bibliography (you should, of course, have already done this).  On Nov. 3 you must turn in an annotated bibliography for the paper.  For each item on your bibliography, you must include brief notes on what each item is about, and how you think it will help you to write your paper.  Naturally, this means that you must have read or at least skimmed the items!


Presentation: During the last two weeks of class (after Thanksgiving), each student will give a short (8-10 minute) presentation of the material that will be covered in the term paper.  Your presentation will be graded on how clearly you explain the topic of your paper, whether you use appropriate visual aids (i.e. powerpoint), whether you stick to the time limit, and how well you answer questions.  Dates for the presentations will be assigned once topics have been assigned, so that we can have coherent sessions of presentations.


Academic Integrity and Inclusion

As a student at IU, you are expected to adhere to the standards detailed in the Code of Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Conduct (Code). Academic misconduct (see here) is defined as any activity that tends to undermine the academic integrity of the institution.  Violations include: cheating, fabrication, plagiarism, interference, violation of course rules, and facilitating academic dishonesty. When you submit an assignment with your name on it, you are signifying that the work contained therein is yours, unless otherwise cited or referenced.  Ignorance of what constitutes academic misconduct or plagiarism is not a valid excuse.  All suspected violations of the Code will be reported to the Office of Student Conduct and handled according to University policies.  Sanctions for academic misconduct may include a failing grade on the assignment, reduction in your final course grade, and a failing grade in the course, among other possibilities.  If you are unsure about the expectations for completing an assignment, be sure to seek clarification from Prof. Deliyannis in advance.


In a writing class, it is particularly important that you understand both why and how you must take responsibility for your own work.  Any ideas or materials taken from another source for either written or oral use must be fully acknowledged in footnotes or endnotes.  Your use of anyone else's (or any AI system's) words, graphic images, calculations, or ideas should be properly cited.  Failure to provide adequate references is considered plagiarism, which is a form of academic misconduct that I am required to handle according to University policies (http://studentcode.iu.edu/responsibilities/academic-misconduct.html).  If you have any question about your use of sources, it is better to be on the safe side and provide a reference.  If you have questions about what constitutes plagiarism and how to avoid it, see https://wts.indiana.edu/writing-guides/plagiarism.html.


AI policy: Generative AI is a tool.  I strongly recommend that you NOT use it, but if you do use it, you are REQUIRED to state your use at the end of the each piece of writing for which you used it, including papers and reading responses ("ChatGPT was used to proofread / sharpen the language / write the introductory paragraph" or whatever you used it for).  IF YOU USE AN AI PROGRAM AND DO NOT ACKNOWLEDGE YOUR USE, YOU WILL BE ASKED TO EXPLAIN YOURSELF TO ME, AND YOU MIGHT BE REPORTED FOR ACADEMIC MISCONDUCT, JUST AS YOU WILL BE REPORTED FOR PLAGIARISM IF YOU DO NOT ACKNOWLEDGE YOUR SOURCES.


If you are experiencing bias in the classroom on the part of any other member, please contact Prof. Deliyannis immediately.  Any act of discrimination or harassment based on race, ethnicity, religious affiliation, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation or disability can be reported through the following options: 1) fill out an online report (can be anonymous) at https://reportincident.iu.edu/; 2) call the Dean of Students Office at (812) 855-8187.



Term paper topics

You will select a paper topics from the list found on the form whose link will be given in Canvas Announcements; each student will write on a different topic.  You are required to choose one of these topics, unless you have a solid background in medieval studies and are willing to do research on your own.  If there is a particular topic that you want to pursue that is not listed here, you must discuss it with Prof. Deliyannis by the second week of classes.


When your topic has been assigned, Prof. Deliyannis will provide you with a bibliography that contains at least one primary source and at least six secondary sources that must be used in your final paper.  At least one of the secondary sources will be a complete book, which you will use for your book report.  In other words, she will do the research for you, but you must do the reading, thinking, and writing based on the sources you have been given.


A good research paper uses primary sources and secondary sources that have been written about the topic.  While reading the secondary sources, you may well find references to more primary sources.  Some of these may not be translated into English; if you have questions, ask!


If you discover additional sources, you are welcome to use those also, but you must use the ones you have been assigned.


The thesis of your paper will be an answer to the question that is the topic.
