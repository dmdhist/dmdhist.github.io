Guidelines and Instructions for your Papers

A.  General information
I want you to learn from these assignments that history is based on evidence, and that it is not permissible to make a statement about something in the past without basing it on some type of evidence from the time period.  Evidence can be written (documentary, literary, historical) or it can be material (archaeological remains, surviving buildings and works of art, etc).  All evidence requires interpretation, of course, and that is what I am asking you to do in your assignments.  In class and through readings, I will provide you with a finite amount of evidence for each assignment, and ask you to interpret only this evidence (and not any other) to produce a piece of historical writing.

There are therefore two parts to each assignment: gathering the evidence, and writing it in smooth prose.  For the first part, you are required to complete and turn in a worksheet has been placed in Canvas Files, "H206 paper worksheet.docx."  The worksheet is designed to help you assemble the pieces of information that you need for your paper.  Once you have done this, you will have assembled most of the information that you need for the paper.  In addition to the worksheet, you must turn in a paper as described below, based on the material on the worksheet.

For each of the assignments, there is a choice of two topics; you may write on whichever you wish, and you do not have to write the same kind of paper every time.  For both topics, you are asked to synthesize information from the primary sources and the material remains to create one short piece of writing.

• The first topic asks you to respond as a historian to a question or questions, in a coherent short essay.  The questions are fairly straightforward; more details about the essays are given below.

• The second topic has you write from the point of view of an inhabitant or visitor to the community, in a historical-fiction style, and has the potential for you to be quite creative.  By all means be as creative as you wish, but YOU WILL NOT BE GRADED ON CREATIVITY, BUT ON HOW WELL YOU CONSTRUCT HISTORY BASED ON THE SOURCES THAT HAVE BEEN PROVIDED!  So your focus MUST be on the sources, and on writing history from the sources.  Let me emphasize again that if you choose to write on the second topic, EVERY ITEM OF INFORMATION IN YOUR PAPER MUST COME FROM THE INFORMATION ON THE WORKSHEET!  Do not assume anything!  THIS TOPIC IS HARDER THAN THE OTHER ONE!

You have been assigned a variety of primary source readings in association with each unit (these can be accessed by the links on the online syllabus).  Some of the readings are about the specific communities we are discussing, but some are not.  In the latter case, the readings contain information about the society that is closely related in time and space to that community, and so you are to use it as evidence for what life might have been like in each community.

Your other sources are the material remains from each community, as presented in class and as found on the associated websites (linked from the online syllabus).

Before you start writing, think about the connections between primary sources, communities, and the historical context in relation to the question.  The connections are there -- you just need to find them to put it all together.  Next, select the passages from the primary sources that you think will help you to answer the question and choose the images that you will use to support your points. Fill out the worksheet with these and the other information.

Papers must be 750-1000 words long, and should be created in MSWord or a Word-readable format as a double-spaced paper (but do not put extra spaces between paragraphs). THE SYSTEM WILL NOT ACCEPT PDFs.  Assignments must be uploaded to the correct Canvas Assignments item, by 12:45 pm (the start of class) on the day that they are due.

Worksheets and papers are evaluated using Turnitin, against web sources and against papers from previous versions of this class.  Do your own work!

It is intellectually dishonest, and against university rules, to copy information from a previous paper, from a book, or from a website, without citing your source -- this is called plagiarism. For more information, see https://wts.indiana.edu/writing-guides/plagiarism.html.  If you plagiarize, submit the same paper as someone else, or are otherwise academically dishonest, I am required to handle this according to University misconduct policies (http://studentcode.iu.edu/responsibilities/academic-misconduct.html), and at a minimum you will fail that assignment. If you are in any doubt, you should cite your source.

AI does a terrible job with writing these papers, and most people who use it fail the assignment because they have not fulfilled the requirements, which, as you will see below, are quite complicated. If you use AI inappropriately, you will also be reported for academic misconduct.

If you need help with your writing, Writing Tutorial Services provides both group and individual instruction for FREE; I strongly encourage you to consult them early in the semester.


B.  Historical argument and evidence
Historical arguments (or any arguments) MUST be based on evidence. Evidence can be a quote from primary source, or it can be a piece of material culture. Whenever you make an argument, or even a statement, you should be able to back it up with a specific piece of evidence.

In a literature class, you might first give a quote from the text and then interpret it ("this means ____"). THIS IS DISCOURAGED IN THIS CLASS! In the type of historical writing that I want you to do, you should make the statement first, and then provide the evidence that backs it up.

So, for example, do not make vague statements like, "Women had low status in the Middle Ages." Instead, you should say, "Women had fewer rights and a lower value in early medieval society. For example, in the Salic Law, it says that stealing a male slave has a penalty of 1400 denarii, whereas stealing a female slave has a penalty of 1200 denarii." Thus, the statement you made has been backed up by a piece of evidence from a primary source. If you can provide two pieces of evidence for the same argument, that's even better!


C.  Grading criteria for these assignments
Your assignments will be graded on a 4.0 scale, which corresponds to letter grades. Thus, a grade of 4.0 is an A, a grade of 3.3 is a B+, a grade of 3.5 is halfway between an A and a B, etc. These are not percentages!

Assignments will be graded as follows:

WORKSHEET: 2.4 points
Historical context and orange terms (0.8 points):
In each paper, you should not be talking about "medieval times", but you should briefly say something about the particular time and place that your paper is about. Look over your class notes and skim the assigned reading in the textbook to refresh your sense of the historical context, and to decide which elements are relevant to what you are writing in your paper. Describe the historical context in a sentence or two, e.g. "This village was occupied in Britain after the Roman government had pulled out; as a result, ______." DO NOT provide a summary of the lectures, this is a common mistake. The historical context should complement your analysis but should never be the focal point. MAKE SURE THAT YOU HAVE A SPECIFIC DATE AND A GEOGRAPHICAL PLACE IN THE FIRST PARAGRAPH OF YOUR PAPER.

Select 3 of the orange terms from the relevant lectures that are appropriate to the way you are writing your paper, and write their definitions. Then, use them appropriately in your paper. PLEASE UNDERLINE OR HIGHLIGHT THESE IN YOUR PAPER.

If you turn in a paper that does not do any of criteria 2-4, the paper will receive an F. In other words, you cannot pass an assignment simply by turning in well-written prose that answers the question, without any of the other requirements.

You must use evidence from three of the assigned primary sources.  "Using evidence" in this case means deriving information directly from a source, quoting or paraphrasing from the source.

5) Organization and style.
You are expected to be able to write correctly.  Your essay should be well-organized, with correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation.  If you have questions, ask us, or ask a writing tutor.

-  Your paper MUST have a clear introduction. Do not simply rephrase the question; instead, provide a short overview of what you will cover in your paper. It is entirely permissible, even encouraged, to conclude your introductory paragraph with the statement "In this paper I will. . .".  Such a statement both helps you to figure out what you are doing in this paper, and helps the reader understand what you are doing throughout the paper.  Such a statement should probably be answering the assigned questions; if what you are doing in the paper doesn't answer the questions, you're not doing the right thing.
-  The body of your paper should support your points with primary sources, material covered in class, and historical context.
