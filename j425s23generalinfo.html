You are a historian!

As a History major, you have studied various different kinds of history, taking classes that were offered by our faculty.  As your capstone experience, you will study whatever aspect of history most interests you!  You will each select a topic, preferably (but not necessarily) building on work done in J300, J400, a 300-level class, or an internship. You will produce a research project that might take the form of a paper, or might be a documentary film, museum exhibit, online production, etc.  You will then make an oral presentation of your project to the other members of the seminar.

 

The purpose of taking this as a seminar class is to allow you to discuss the research process with other students, and to support each other while thinking about issues related to research and presentation.

 

While the class is led by Prof. Deliyannis, you will identify a faculty member in the History Department to serve as a consultant for your project, and you are required to meet with that faculty member at least three times.

 

Learning outcomes

A successful student will demonstrate, by the end of the semester:

• the ability to frame a research question, identify primary sources that could be used to answer it and secondary sources that provide a historiographical framework

• the ability to produce an argument based on different types of appropriately selected primary and/or secondary sources.

• the ability to present analysis and research in a well-crafted and coherent form, as a research paper, video, exhibit, film, or other long format.

• the ability to present historical information and make historical arguments orally

Attendance is required, and will be taken in every class.  You are allowed one free absence (i.e. no excuse needed).  Participation means having done the assignment for the day, and taking an active part in discussion.  If you are sick, in covid isolation or quarantine, or have another kind of emergency, please send me an email and your attendance will be excused.   

 

One of the advantages of attending a research university is that we have a large faculty with expertise in a broad range of historical times and periods, and they are here for you!  You will identify a faculty consultant for your project in the first week, and you must meet with that person 3 times during the semester; after each meeting, you must submit a brief report.

 

Your research project will be on a topic of your choice.  It might be a 15-20-page paper (double-spaced, 12-pt font, 1" margins).  Or, if you have the skills to produce another type of project in which you display an argument and the fruits of your research (a video, exhibit, etc.), you may take that option.  In either case, all notes and bibliography will use either MLA or Chicago Manual of Style (you can access it through IUCAT) for references.

 

Most of the assignments for this class revolve around your research project; some of them are graded, as above; most will be submitted on Canvas on the due date, except where otherwise noted. 

 

For several class meetings, we will be workshopping your research, so you will need to bring a laptop or other device on which you can work.

Academic Integrity and Inclusion

As a student at IU, you are expected to adhere to the standards detailed in the Code of Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Conduct (Code). Academic misconduct (see here) is defined as any activity that tends to undermine the academic integrity of the institution.  Violations include: cheating, fabrication, plagiarism, interference, violation of course rules, and facilitating academic dishonesty.  When you submit an assignment with your name on it, you are signifying that the work contained therein is yours, unless otherwise cited or referenced.

 

In a research class, it is particularly important that you understand both why and how you must take responsibility for your own work.  Any ideas or materials taken from another source for either written or oral use must be fully acknowledged in footnotes or endnotes.  All suspected violations of the Code will be reported to the Dean of Students and handled according to University policies.  Sanctions for academic misconduct may include a failing grade on the assignment, reduction in your final course grade, and a failing grade in the course, among other possibilities.  If you are unsure about the expectations for completing an assignment, be sure to seek clarification from Prof. Deliyannis in advance.
