Example:

 

Edict of Milan:  The Edict of Milan was issued by Constantine and his co-emperor in the east in 313.  It declared that Christianity was legal in the Roman empire, which ended the persecution that had been begun by Diocletian.  This allowed Christianity to become a mainstream, and eventually the dominant religion, in the Roman empire.

For each selection, you must describe what the text means, what its historical context is (using specific terms such as those found on the list of terms), and how it relates to one of the sites (Ravenna, West Stow, Wharram Percy) we have studied.  You should be writing 1-2 paragraphs for each answer.

 

 

Example:

 

"And he [the bailiff] ought to sow the lands, and be over the plowers and harrowers at the time of each sowing. And he ought to make all the boon-tenants and customary-tenants who are bound and accustomed to come, do so, to do the work they ought to do. And in haytime he ought to be over the mowers, the making, the carrying, and in August assemble the reapers and the boon-tenants and the laborers and see that the corn be properly and cleanly gathered; and early and late watch so that nothing be stolen or eaten by beasts or spoilt. And he ought to tally with the reeve all the seed, and boon-work, and customs, and labor, which ought to be done in the manor throughout the year, and what it amounts to the bailiff tallies and accounts for, and they ought to answer on the account for the rest."

 

Answer:

 

This selection describes farm work on a later medieval manor.  The bailiff is appointed to be the person who gets all the peasants in the village to do the farmwork in the fields together.  Work on a later medieval manor was generally done communally; that is, all the peasants worked together on everyone's fields and then each took the produce grown on his particular part of the fields.  This was because the agricultural techniques, such as the use of the mouldboard plow, required sharing the large number of animals required to pull the plow.  The lord of the manor took his share, too; it was part of the adminstrators' jobs to make sure the shares were given out properly. 

 

Wharram Percy is an example of this kind of village.  At Wharram Percy, all the villagers lived together in the middle of the fields; the lord (and his appointees like the bailiff) lived in the two manor houses, which were larger and had more buildings for storage and crafts than the peasants' houses.
