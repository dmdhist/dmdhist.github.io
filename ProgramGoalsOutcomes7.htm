Students graduating with a History Major will pursue the following Goals by achieving the following Learning Outcomes

 

 

Program Goals

Associated Learning Outcomes

Historical Knowledge

Students will understand the forces, ideas and actions that have shaped human history.

·      They will demonstrate a broad knowledge about historical events and complex causality in more than one geographical, chronological, or thematic field.

·      They will be able to evaluate historical events from the varied perspective[s] of the people of the time.

Historical Knowing

Students will understand how people have used history in the past and continue to use it, and will be able to evaluate that usage.

·      They will be acquainted with how history works as a discipline, about the different types of history, and that ideas about the past have changed over time

·      They will be able to establish appropriate emotional distance in thinking about the past.

·      They will be equipped to distinguish responsible invocations of history in public discourse from specious or misleading ones.

Historical Argument

Students will understand how historical arguments work and will be able to produce historical arguments based on evidence.

·      They will recognize that historians make arguments about the  past, and be able to identify, evaluate and critique different interpretations based on the evidence.

·      They will understand the fundamental importance of primary sources to historical practice

·      They will be able to evaluate primary sources, taking into consideration authorship, audience, dating, and purpose, and that fact that such sources might contradict each other

·      They will be able to make a historical argument, either written or oral, using evidence from appropriate sources

Historical Research
Students will know how to conduct historical research and present it in writing, orally, or in some other appropriate way.

·      They will be able to ask a good historical research question and know how to find and use evidence to address it.

·      Given a research question by someone else, they will be able to design and carry out a research program to answer it.

·      They will be able to present their research in a well-crafted form, as a short or long paper, an oral report, a poster, a film or documentary, a museum exhibit, a conference paper.

Professional Skills

Students will understand how skills developed through the study of history can be used in a variety of contexts and jobs.

 

·      Writing skills: Students will be able to present their ideas clearly in writing, adapting their writing to the format of the genre required (report, summary, overview, long or short paper)

·      Research skills: Students be able to evaluate potential sources of information, to distinguish good sources from bad, to extract usable material and to shape the information into coherent knowledge

·      Public presentation skills: Students will be able to present their ideas clearly in an oral form appropriate to the setting (conference, presentation, pitch) taking into account the purpose of the presentation and the audience

·      Cultural Literacy: Students will possess common historical knowledge that will help them understand and join in educated discourse.

·      Socio-Cultural Competence: Students will be aware that individuals have different perspectives that seem reasonable to them and will be able to respect those perspectives enabling constructive dialogue across cultural and social lines.

·      Critical Thinking: Students will not simply accept data as givens, but will be able to interrogate it, analyze it, evaluate it, and then put it to use.
