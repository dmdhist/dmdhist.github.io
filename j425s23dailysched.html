Proposed Schedule


Jan. 10    Introduction

            Assignment (due Jan. 17): upload your J400 paper to Canvas Assignments


Jan. 17    Choosing a research topic

            Meet with Prof. Deliyannis either in this class meeting or in office hours (signup for a time will be posted in Canvas announcements) to discuss your research topic and faculty consultant.

            Assignment (to be completed by Jan. 24):  after your meeting, upload to Canvas your research topic, phrased as a question that you hope to answer

            Assignment (due Jan. 31):  contact your consultant, set up a first appointment to be held by Jan. 30; submit the form about your meeting


Jan. 24    Using library resources 

            In-class discussion about, and work on bibliographies

            Assignment (due Jan. 31):  Upload to Canvas a preliminary bibliography for your research project.  You should certainly have consulted your faculty consultant about this.  You should include both primary and secondary sources on your bibliography, and you must include at least one scholarly monograph.  The bibliography should be formatted correctly using either MLA or Chicago format.

                  Your bibliography must contain, at a minimum:

                  - one scholarly monograph

                  - eight articles published in academic history journals (if in doubt, ask)

                  - any other resources that are useful

                  - primary sources consisting of a database or a large set of texts or images


Jan. 31    Making your own research work plan and strategy

            In-class creation of work plan and a work log     

            Assignment (due Feb. 7):  This assignment should be done on Powerpoint or an equivalent, and uploaded to Canvas.  Make a slide for one of your primary sources, with the following information:

                  - what it is

                  - how you are accessing it (i.e. book, online, archive, etc.)

                  - what language was the original, how you are reading it

                  - who wrote/made it, when, where

                  - why it was made

                  - what its presumed audience was

            On a second slide, put

                  - a representative sample (a paragraph, a newspaper item, an image) to share with the class

            Assignment (due Feb. 7):  pick a scholarly article from your bibliography, and upload its PDF to Canvas "Discussions" -> "Scholarly article upload", give the PDF the name "YourLastName".

 

Feb. 7    Primary sources

            In-class discussion of primary sources; presentation of your primary source

            Assignment (due Feb. 14):  read your article and complete the Canvas assignment "Scholarly article analysis", in which you note:

                  - the thesis

                  - the primary sources from the footnotes

                  - whether the author is arguing against historical ideas from other historians

                  - whether you think the argument is coherent

            You will do the same thing with another student's article, in class.


Feb. 14    Secondary sources

            In-class discussion of scholarly articles, based on your assigned readings

            Assignment (due Feb. 28):  make an appointment to meet a second time with your faculty consultant, and submit a report by Feb. 28


Feb. 21    Meet with consultant, work on gathering and reading your research materials

            No in-class meeting

            Assignment (due Feb. 28):  Upload to Canvas an annotated bibliography of everything on your list, both primary and secondary sources.  For each item, you write brief notes on what it is about, and how you think it will help you to write your paper.  Naturally, this means that you must have read or at least skimmed the items!


Feb. 28    Historical context

            In-class discussion about the historical context of your projects:  what's the scope, what kinds of information do you need to include?

            Individual meetings, if desired, with the remaining time

            Assignment (due Mar. 7):  present an oral book report about one scholarly monograph on your bibliography.  Your book oral report should take no more than 5 minutes (practice!  and be concise).  In it you should briefly explain (a) what the subject and thesis of the book are; (b) whether you can tell if the author is arguing against other theories; (c) what primary sources the author uses in the book; and (d) whether you found it informative and convincing.   THERE IS NO WRITTEN COMPONENT TO THIS ASSIGNMENT.


Mar. 7    Book reports

            Class presentations of book reports


SPRING BREAK


Mar. 21    Meet with consultant;  work on your paper

            No in-class meeting

            Assignment (due April 4):  make an appointment for a third meeting with your faculty consultant, and submit a report by Apr. 4.  It might be useful to discuss your thesis at this meeting.


Mar. 28    Work on your paper

            No in-class meeting

            Assignment (due Apr. 4):  submit a 2-paragraph research report, that is, an assessment of where you are, compared to your work plan.  This assessment should include:

                  - have you done what you planned to do?  if not, why not?  assess yourself!

                  - how will you get back on track?

                  - what was unexpected when you started working on your project?


Apr. 4    How are we doing?

            Class discussion of research reports, presentations, footnote references, other issues


Apr. 11    Finish up your draft

            No in-class meeting

            Assignment (due Apr. 18):  Upload a full draft of your project to Canvas "Discussions"   -> "Project draft upload", please name it with your last name.  This has to be a complete draft, from thesis to conclusion, because we will be doing peer review in class this week.


Apr. 18    Peer review of drafts

            In-class peer review of a partner's draft, and discussion


Apr. 25    Presentations

            First day of in-class project presentations; dates will be assigned in the third week of classes.

            - You will give a 10-12 minute presentation of the material that will be covered in your project .  Your presentation will be graded on how clearly you explain the topic of your paper, whether you use appropriate visual aids (powerpoint or similar), whether you stick to the time limit, and how well you answer questions. 


May 2, 3:00-5:00 pm    Presentations [note: this is the assigned final exam time slot]

            Second day of in-class project presentations.
