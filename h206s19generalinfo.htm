Course Description

The European Middle Ages spans more than a thousand years, from approximately 400 to 1500 AD, and includes the many different political, cultural, and ethnic communities of Europe and the Mediterranean. There was not one medieval civilization, but many medieval civilizations, related in some ways to each other, but distinct and constantly changing. This class will be an introduction to the history of the Middle Ages through its culture and ways of life.  


Because the period to be covered is so vast, we will focus our attention on six moments in time and space that are representative of some of the communities of the Middle Ages. We will look at villages and cathedral towns, monasteries and manor houses and cities, and for each, we will consider who lived in the community, what activities they took part in, what the community looked like physically, and what aspects of medieval life, politics, and culture are represented there.



Learning Objectives

A successful student will demonstrate, by the end of the semester:

• knowledge of key events, peoples, institutions, and ideas that shaped the cultures of Europe between 300 and 1500.

• facility in reading and understanding primary sources, including both texts and material culture

• the ability to deploy relevant evidence, in an effective and persuasive manner in writing, including:

     - drawing inferences from primary sources and relating that information to broad course themes

     - quoting appropriately and contextually

     - using evidence from primary sources to answer particular questions about life and culture in the past

Structure

The class will consist primarily of lectures; if you have a question at any time during the lecture, feel free to raise your hand and ask.  If you find something confusing, probably many other people do too, and they will appreciate your question also.


If at any time outside of class you have questions or wish to talk to me about any aspect of the class, or about history, or about anything else, please come to my office hours, as listed above.  If these times are not convenient, I will be happy to schedule an appointment.  I am also very accessible via e-mail.


Course requirements

                5%       Attendance at lecture

                5%       Question group (see below)

              40%       Four short papers/exercises  (10% each)

              20%       Midterm exam

              30%       Final exam

           100%       TOTAL

Attendance will be taken in lecture; your attendance will be worth 5% of your final grade.  You will be allowed two 'free' absences.  If you are sick or have a valid emergency, email to let Prof. Deliyannis know and your absence will be excused (i.e. not count as one of your two 'free' absences).


In the third week of the semester, each of you will be assigned to a group made up of five or six people.  Each group will be assigned one class day.  On your assigned day, you will sit in the front row of the classroom.  At some point during the lecture you must ask a question.  The question can be one of clarification, a request for further information, a question about something you read and didn't understand, or anything that relates in some way to the lecture.  If you ask your question, you will receive an A for this component of the course; if you do not ask a question (or if you miss your assigned day), you will earn an F.  You should certainly feel free to ask questions when it is not your turn to sit in the front; but this way everyone is guaranteed the opportunity to ask at least one question.


Short (750-1000 words) papers are assigned for each of the six units. THE FIRST PAPER, ON RAVENNA, IS REQUIRED. Then, of the remaining five papers, you must write three of them, for a total of four. You may choose the topics that most interest you, or the ones that fit best with your schedule. All of the papers have two possible topics, you may write whichever you like. THE MIDTERM AND FINAL EXAMS WILL COVER MATERIAL FROM ALL UNITS, EVEN THOSE FOR WHICH YOU DO NOT WRITE THE PAPER.


For each paper, an essay question or questions have been assigned; you should answer the question specifically by making references to the texts that are the outside readings (and refer to them by name in your paper).  Guidelines for the papers can be found at this link. Papers are due at 1:00 pm on the day they are due.  You must upload an electronic version (.doc, .docx, or .rtf; no .pdf) of your essay to the correct entry on Canvas Assignments.  If you send it late, you must email it to Prof. Deliyannis, who will then open Canvas so that you can submit it there.  Late papers will be marked down one-half letter grade for every 24-hour period that they are late (this includes weekend days); after eight days, they will not be accepted, period.


You never know what crisis will befall you during the semester; it is strongly suggested that you turn in the first three papers rather than waiting until the end of the semester.  I cannot tell you how many students have waited until the end of the semester to write papers, only to discover that they had more to do at the end of the semester than they had realized.


It is intellectually dishonest, and against university rules, to copy information from a previous paper, from a book, or from a website, without citing your source -- this is called plagiarism. For more information, see https://wts.indiana.edu/writing-guides/plagiarism.html.  If you plagiarize, submit the same paper as someone else, or are otherwise academically dishonest, you will be reported to the Office of Student Ethics, and you will fail that assignment.  If you are in any doubt, you should cite your source.


The midterm and final exam will be short-answer tests. Questions will address material covered in lectures and the textbook, and from the primary source readings. You are responsible for reading and understanding all of the primary sources, even if they belong to a paper that you choose not to write. A study guide with information for the midterm will be posted two weeks prior to the exam, and the Course Assistants will run review sessions before both the midterm and the final exams.



Extra Credit

If you choose to write more than four papers, we will either drop the lowest paper grade, or you will be given extra credit of up to 2% on your semester average for each extra paper.  You will receive 1% extra for a B or B+ paper, and 2% for an A- or A paper.  We will calculate your grade to your best advantage.


There will be other opportunities to earn extra credit during the semester, by attending lectures held at IU.  These will be announced via email through Canvas, and also in class.  If you attend such an event and write up a one-page, double-spaced summary of it, you will earn 2% on your semester grade.


You are allowed a maximum of 4% extra credit altogether.
