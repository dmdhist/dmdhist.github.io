General Instructions for Debates

Your debate fulfils several learning objectives for this class:

- it is an opportunity for you to examine one issue in some depth.  I do NOT expect that you have any background in formal debate.  I DO expect that for your debate you will be well-prepared, knowledgable about the historical background and the issue itself, and able to argue a case. You are not required to do huge amounts of research, but you should at a minimum be very familiar with everything provided on the debate webpage, including the primary and secondary sources that have been provided.
- it will provide you with an opportunity to give an oral presentation on a subject for which you have prepared; it will require that you be able to explain and argue historical concepts orally.
- it allows you to work in a small group

Since this is a group activity, you are required to meet with member(s) of your debate team at least one time prior to the class meeting in which the debate will take place (this can be in-person or on zoom).  The instructor will help to facilitate this meeting.

The debate will take the place of lecture on a given topic for that day; thus, debaters will be required to provide background material on their subjects (from the textbook, for example) as part of their arguments.  Each background item should take a minimum of 3 minutes to deliver.  Do not assume that your audience knows anything!  You are expected to make a powerpoint to illustrate your points; you are welcome to use maps or images that have already been used in class.  If you are in doubt as to whether you are clear enough, practice on a friend and ask if he/she understands!

Each debate assignment will consist of eight formal, pre-arranged parts: 3 items of background information, presentation of the proposition, three arguments, and conclusion.  You may divide preparation of these up as your team chooses, but you do not know which parts you will be called upon to present, so you will have to be ready to do all of them.  In addition to the presentation and argumentation stages of the debate, there will also be counter-argument for which each student is required to speak at least once, and there will be questions from the rest of the class.  You will be graded INDIVIDUALLY on how well you do on the part that you presented, AND how you do in counter-arguments and questions.  I will NOT be grading you on your formal debating skills, so if you have never debated before, that is not a problem.

Note that in order to create a solid argument for your side, you will have to figure out what the opposing side's arguments might be so that you can plan what your counter-argument will be.  You will be expected to present a solid and detailed argument for your side, using background and other information.

People often ask what the difference between the presentation of the proposition and the arguments is.  The presentation of the proposition should include a statement of what the proposition is, an explanation of what is involved, and a brief summary of the overall theme of how it is going to be argued.  Do not go into detail about the individual arguments at this time; that will be done when the arguments are presented.

The structure of each debate will be the following:

I.  Background material
               A.  Team 1
               B.  Team 2
II.  Presentation of the proposition
               A.  Team 1
               B.  Team 2
III.  Main arguments:  four for each side, in detail, including quotes from primary sources
               A.  Team 1
               B.  Team 2
IV.  Counter-arguments - cannot be prepared in advance
               A.   Team 1
               B.   Team 2
V.  Conclusion: state why your argument is preferable
VI. Questions from the rest of the class
VII.  Vote by the class (not part of the grade!)
