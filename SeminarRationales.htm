Seminars - Rationales

 

a.  Status Quo.

Pro:

- J300 and J400 give majors the opportunity to take small classes

- J400 gives majors a course of their own, without non-majors.

- J300 and J400 provide training in research and writing

Con:

- Because J300 is a writing intensive course shared with non-majors, the class doesn’t fully prepare majors for the research expected of them in J400.

-  Majors very often don't find topics that are of interest to them, or in which they have any prior course experience.   There are particular complaints about the lack of U.S. courses.

- There is not enough time in J400 classes for students both to master a body of historical content and to write a full-fledged research paper.

- The status quo will not give greater attention to majors.

- The status quo will not help give students a greater sense of cohesion and sequence in the major.

- The status quo will not improve teaching opportunities for faculty.

- The status quo will not help maintain or increase teaching opportunities for graduate students.

- The status quo—part of a major much like every other history major around the country—will not raise our profile on the campus and beyond.

 

b.  Status Quo, broader topics

Pro:

- Broader topics increase odds that J300 and J400 will include subjects of particular interest to majors.

Con:

- Broader topics will not solve other problems with current requirement noted above.

- Broader topics will not necessarily meet demand for more U.S. history.

- Faculty may not want to teach broader topics.

 

c.  Status Quo, plus one

Pro:

- Third seminar—students’ choice of J300 or J400—will give majors more opportunities to learn and practice skills in a small setting.

- Third seminar will build greater sense of community among majors, particularly if they choose major-only J400.

- Requirement focuses more department resources on the needs of majors.

- Requirement will increase faculty opportunities to teach in small setting.

- Requirement demonstrates department’s commitment to majors and response to declining numbers of majors and enrollments.

Con:

- Majors who take two J300s still won’t necessarily have training in research to prepare them for J400.

- Requirement doesn’t address problem of covering both content and research paper in J400.

- Requirement doesn’t increase cohesion and sequence in the major.  If students take second J300 after J400, major can become still less coherent.

- Additional seminar may mean dropping too many lecture courses, with loss of subject matter and possibly enrollments.  Based on current offerings of J300 & J400, department would need to offer 9 more seminars in order to meet requirement.

- Requirement does not address retention or increase of graduate student teaching opportunities.

- Requirement is arguably “more of the same,” an incremental change that does not raise department’s profile.

 

d.  J300, J400, and J450

Pro:

- New capstone seminar will enable majors to develop skills in research and presentation.

- By allowing both research papers (“senior essays”) and projects such as exhibits, J450 will better serve the range of majors.

- By increasing the proportion of the major devoted to active research in the broadest sense, J450 reflects literature on importance of research for undergraduates.

- Combination of J400 and J450 will help majors apply for jobs and professional school: they will be able to say that they have created, managed, and defended successfully a major intellectual project across the full span of an academic year.

- By building on J300 and J400, seminar increases cohesion and progression in the major.

- By removing from J400 the requirement to finish a research paper (or project), J450 will make J400 a more successful course, in which instructors can both teach content and help students prepare for J450.

- Another small course for majors only, J450 will help build community among majors.

- Addition of J450 increases the department’s commitment to serving majors.

- J450 will increase the range of teaching opportunities for faculty without requiring so many cancelled lecture courses.

- J450 will increase teaching opportunities for advanced graduate students.  This class focuses on work they are most able to do: supervise papers, lead discussion, and facilitate presentations.

- New seminar sequence, especially in conjunction with required gateway course, will create a distinctive history major that raises the department’s profile and reflects a committed response to declining majors and enrollments.

- J450 reflects the emphasis on “high-impact” learning in the campus Strategic Plan.

Con:

- Third required seminar may be more than some students will want.

- Some majors may not want to carry out a major paper or project.

- Requirement may drive down number of majors.

- Graduate students may face challenges teaching this class.

- Faculty oversight of graduate student teachers will consume more faculty resources.

- Faculty may not want to serve as advisers for projects.

- Despite use of graduate students, staffing of course could strain faculty resources.

- Some majors may not want to carry out a major paper or project.

 

e. Optional J300, require J400 and J450

Pro:

- By making J300 optional, we will avoid losing majors who would be put off by having three required seminars (J300, J400, & J450) and perhaps a required gateway course as well

- Because students in the College must meet Intensive Writing requirement, we will still have many majors taking J300 anyway.

 

Con:

- We will probably lose some enrollments in J300.

- We will be sending the wrong signal about how much preparation students need to excel at the 400 level

- We will be ignoring complaints that our students don’t have enough instruction and practice in skills for work at the 400 level

- If we loosen distribution requirements, three and even four required courses (gateway, J300, J400, & J450) are neither excessive nor out of line with requirements of other College majors.

 

Honors.

None of the alternatives listed above implies the abandonment of our senior Honors program, but the J450 requirement would perhaps require some refocusing of Honors.  Although inspired in part by the two-semester work in Honors, the J400/J450 sequence is not a duplication of the program.  The majors who currently take Honors work at a higher level, particularly in dealing with the nature of history; none of the alternatives envisions raising the level of difficulty of J400.  Similarly, J450 should not require the same level of difficulty and intensity as the Honors paper.  Honors should continue to attract our best and most ambitious majors, including those interested in pursuing graduate study in history.  The Honors notation should continue to be a desirable incentive.
